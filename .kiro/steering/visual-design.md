# MediCap visual design direction

Use a **light clinical-editorial** system across both handwritten clients in `docs/` and `public/`.

## Required direction
- Use white and warm-neutral surfaces with thin gray rules and generous, intentional whitespace.
- Use IBM Plex Sans for headings, body, and controls; reserve IBM Plex Mono for room IDs, timestamps, indices, and compact operational metadata.
- Use blue for navigation and primary actions, green for available/success, amber for near-capacity warnings, and red for full/critical states. Never rely on color alone; retain text and icons.
- Prefer flat ruled sections, compact metadata, status rails, and clear operational hierarchy over a repeated equal-card kit.
- Keep corners restrained (roughly 3–6px), shadows rare, and motion limited to short functional transitions with a reduced-motion path.
- Apply shared visual changes to both `docs/` and `public/` explicitly; neither surface is generated from the other.

## Avoid generic AI-dashboard styling
Do not add aurora backgrounds, neon gradients, glass blur, glowing shadows, purple/cyan startup palettes, gratuitous pills, oversized rounded cards, staggered card entrances, fake terminal chrome, decorative labels, or multiple unrelated typefaces.

## Reference discipline
When public references are requested, apply `.kiro/skills/design-reference-toolkit/SKILL.md`. Select one primary principle and at most two supporting principles. Borrow abstract solutions only; never copy source HTML, CSS, JavaScript, assets, copy, exact palette, type pairing, composition, claims, or signature choreography.

## Verification
Check 320px, intermediate, and desktop layouts; keyboard and focus behavior; written status labels; contrast; reduced motion; factual capacity calculations; and consistency between authored sources.
